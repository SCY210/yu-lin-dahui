# Notification enrollment points

Policy `2026-10-13-v1` awards 10 doubles season points once per account after explicit enrollment and push-service acceptance. A member with an existing subscription can claim from notification settings after reading the same rules. Legacy clients and silent status checks do not enroll an account in this policy.

After the award, explicitly disabling any registered device in the site's settings deducts 30 doubles season points. Returning to the site (including foregrounding it) also deducts those same 30 points when the browser reports `denied` or `default` after a known enabled device. These paths share one pending deduction per account. Re-enabling through the website and passing push-service verification refunds exactly that pending 30-point deduction; it never grants the initial 10-point award again. Each refund references its original penalty audit ID (`refundOf`) and restores the original player and month, including across quarter/year boundaries. Concurrent or repeated requests cannot refund a penalty twice. Another disable after a refund opens a new 30-point deduction; repeated reports while a deduction remains pending do not stack penalties. The net notification adjustment is +10 while restored, or -20 while a penalty is pending, and balances may be negative. Awards and penalties belong to the month of each action; refunds belong to the original deduction month, feeding the matching quarterly and annual doubles boards. They do not change singles points, match counts, wins, cultivation or grouping strength.

The notification prompt and settings disclose awards, penalties and refunds before enrollment; disabling requires confirmation. Notifications remain optional. Device-specific logout, provider expiry, delivery errors and subscription cleanup do not deduct points. Browser/system permission loss is checked on entry without asking for permission automatically. A missing subscription alone is not evidence of permission loss. Some operating-system switches do not affect browser-visible permission and cannot be detected. Disabling notification categories does not remove a device subscription.

Server authentication determines the account. Device writes and immutable audit deltas share an optimistic club-revision transaction, with retry on conflicts. Device ownership is checked again within the penalty transaction. An opaque 256-bit device token in a Secure/HttpOnly/SameSite=Lax cookie (up to one year) is matched against a server-side SHA-256 receipt bound to the account and subscription; raw tokens are never returned in JSON or stored in the audit ledger. A new browser without a receipt or an existing owned subscription is not penalized. Existing enabled browsers obtain a receipt during the normal granted-permission status check. Logout and provider cleanup deactivate the receipt. The server trusts an authenticated browser permission report; it does not have OS attestation. Enrollment validates subscription keys and requires successful push-service acceptance before the first reward and each refund; this is not proof that the operating system displayed the notification. Provider failure grants or refunds no points. Previously recorded rewards and penalties remain unchanged and are eligible for the same paired refund flow.

The penalty is a product rule requested by the owner, not a legal compliance guarantee. Where personal-data processing relies on consent, a penalty for withdrawing consent can undermine freely given consent; disclosure alone does not resolve that issue.

Tests use synthetic members, in-memory SQLite and a mocked push provider. Never test incentive accounting against production members.
